Reach

A step-by-step guide to running Reach — setting up the telephone responders who use it, enrolling the Hand handsets that receive alerts, and keeping both working.

What is Reach?

Reach is the public-facing front end of the intergroup's member data. Someone who needs a 12th-step call signs in with Google, Microsoft, Apple or Facebook — or with an email and password — and gets a mobile-friendly list of the nearest members willing to take the call.

Reach is also the server side of Hand, the phone app certified telephone responders carry. Reach enrols the handsets, stores the alerts and pushes them out. Those two jobs are why this guide has two halves: managing responders and setting up devices.

⚠️
Reach has no member list of its own. Everybody it knows about comes from the shared member records (the Unity plugin), and everything it shows is governed by what is ticked there. If someone can't sign in, can't be found, or isn't getting alerts, the answer is almost always on their member record — not in Reach.

How the Pieces Fit Together

Five things are involved. You only administer the first three, but it helps to know what each one contributes.

PieceWhat it does
Member records
(Unity / Intergroup › Members)		 The one place a person exists. Their email, mobile, whether they are a 12th stepper, whether they are a telephone responder, and how far through certification they have got.
Reach
(the website)		 The sign-in pages and the finder at /reach/find, plus the admin screens under the Reach menu.
Hand
(the phone app)		 The duty handset. It receives alerts raised for the rota and rings like a call. Reach enrols and revokes handsets; the app itself is installed on the responder's own phone.
Trusted
(the rota, optional)		 The weekly shift rota. When it is active, responders can sign up for shifts from inside Reach, and Trusted is what raises "shift uncovered" alerts.
Scrutiny
(the audit log)		 Records who looked at whose personal details, and when. Nothing to configure here — but it is why several Reach screens are gated behind a personal-data permission.
💡
Reach and Hand are not the same audience. The website admits a 12th stepper or a certified telephone responder. Hand admits certified telephone responders and nobody else. See Roles & Who Gets What — this catches people out more than anything else in the plugin.

Roles & Who Gets What

Three separate settings on a member record decide what a person can do. They are independent — one person can be any combination — and getting them confused is the single most common support question.

SettingWhat it meansReach websiteHand app
Is Twelfth Stepper? Willing to be contacted directly for a 12th-step call. Has an area they cover and the forms of contact they accept. Can sign in, and appears in the finder. No
Telephone Responder Takes turns answering the helpline. Only these members can be put on the Trusted rota. Can sign in. Only once Certified.
Responder Certification How far through the certification process a responder has got — see below. Makes no difference. Certified and nothing else.
⚠️
Hand's gate is stricter than the website's, on purpose. A 12th stepper with no responder role can sign in to Reach and will be refused by Hand. A responder who is Applied, In Training or Pending will also be refused. Someone who has not been cleared to take helpline calls must not be sent a caller's details.

Reach re-asks this question on every single request a handset makes, and again when an alert is about to be delivered — it is not frozen in at enrolment. So a responder whose certification is changed, or who is removed from the member list, stops receiving alerts by themselves, without anybody remembering to go and revoke their handset.

Members are not stored in Reach — they live on the shared member record, a form organised into groups (About, Home, Service, GDPR Compliance). The fields that matter to Reach and Hand are in About and Service. A switch is a yes/no toggle, and * marks a required field:

  1. Go to Intergroup › Members and open the member (or Add New for somebody who isn't recorded yet).
  2. In the About group, fill in the Personal Email and Mobile Number. The email is not optional here — it is the identity Reach and Hand sign them in with.
  3. In the Service group, switch Telephone Responder to On.

🚫
Personal Email must be the member's own address. An intergroup address (anything on aa-bristol.org) is refused when saving, and rightly so: those forward to whoever currently holds a position, so it would sign the wrong person in and stop working the moment that person rotates out.
💡
Making somebody a responder does not make them findable by the public — that is Is Twelfth Stepper? in the Home group, along with the areas they cover. Tick both for a member who does both jobs.

The Certification Stages

Certification is a single choice on the member record with five values. Only the last one opens the Hand app.

StageMeaningCan use Hand
NoneNothing recorded — either not a responder at all, or not yet started.No
AppliedHas put their name forward.No
In TrainingWorking through the training.No
PendingTraining done, waiting on final sign-off.No
CertifiedCleared to take helpline calls.Yes
⚠️
Moving somebody back from Certified takes effect immediately — their handset stops being sent alerts at its very next request. You do not need to revoke it as well, though you may still want to if the phone itself has gone (see Revoking a Handset).

A member who is not flagged as a telephone responder has no certification at all: the field is hidden, and hidden means None. Turning the responder toggle off is therefore a complete way to stand somebody down.

How a Responder Signs In

There are no WordPress user accounts behind any of this. Reach proves who somebody is by their email address and then looks that address up in the member records. Two ways to prove it:

1. Sign in with Google, Microsoft, Apple or Facebook

The usual route, and the one to encourage — nothing to remember and no password for you to manage. The provider verifies the email, Reach checks it against the member records, and that is the whole exchange. The buttons that appear on /reach/signin are whichever providers have been configured under Settings.

⚠️
The address they sign in with must match the Personal Email on their member record. Somebody whose record says sarah@gmail.com but who signs in with a work Microsoft account is, as far as Reach can tell, a stranger — and is turned away.

2. Sign in with an email and password

For anyone who has none of those accounts, or would rather not use them. Nobody sets a password for them; they set their own:

  1. The responder opens /reach/reset and enters their email address.
  2. If that address belongs to an eligible member, a one-time link is emailed to it. (The page says the same thing either way, deliberately — it must not reveal who is and isn't a member.)
  3. They follow the link to /reach/set-password, choose a password, and are signed in straight away.
  4. From then on they can sign in with email and password on /reach/signin, and can use the same credentials in the Hand app.
💡
The same /reach/reset page is what a responder uses if they forget the password later — there is no separate "forgotten password" screen, and nothing for you to reset by hand.

When a sign-in is refused

Reach never returns a raw error to somebody signing in; it puts them back on the sign-in page with a readable message. The three you may be asked about:

What they seeWhat it actually means
Not eligible / we can't find youThe verified email matches no member, or matches a member who is neither a 12th stepper nor a telephone responder. Fix the member record.
We need your email addressThey declined to share their email with Facebook, so Facebook handed over an anonymised relay address that can't receive mail. They should sign in again and share it, or use another provider.
Sign-in failedThe attempt expired or went wrong in transit. Trying again usually fixes it.

Apple's private relay addresses are not treated this way — Apple genuinely forwards mail through them, so they are accepted.

Shift Sign-Up

When the Trusted rota plugin is active, a signed-in responder sees a Shift sign-up button on the Reach menu page and can put themselves on an open shift from their phone at /reach/shifts.

There is nothing to switch on: the button appears when Trusted is present and is simply absent when it isn't. Trusted enforces its own rules — responders only, one person per shift — so a member who has just been flagged as a responder can sign up immediately, and one who has been stood down cannot.

💡
Shift sign-up and Hand alerts are different things and have different gates. Sign-up needs the Telephone Responder toggle; Hand additionally needs Certified.

Standing a Responder Down

What to do depends on why. In every case the change takes effect at the responder's next request — there is no cache to clear and no separate Reach record to tidy up.

SituationDo thisWhat happens
Certification has lapsed, or is under review Change Responder Certification away from Certified. Hand stops delivering alerts to them at once. They can still sign in to the Reach website.
Taking a break from the helpline Switch Telephone Responder off. As above, plus they disappear from the rota's assignment list.
Lost or replaced phone Revoke the handset on Reach › Hand devices. That one handset's access is cut immediately; the responder keeps everything else.
Left the fellowship / record deleted Delete the member record as usual. Reach revokes all their handsets and deletes their stored password automatically.
🚫
If you suspect a handset or token has been compromised more widely, rotating the site's authentication salts invalidates every enrolled handset at once and everyone re-enrols. That is a site-administrator job, not something on a Reach screen — but it is the big red switch if you ever need it.

What Hand Is

Hand is the app a certified telephone responder installs on their own phone. It is the duty handset: when an alert is raised — a shift nobody has covered, a caller waiting — every enrolled handset rings, like a call rather than a polite notification, and whoever picks it up acknowledges it.

Hand
Helpline shift uncovered
Tonight 22:00–08:00 has nobody signed up.
Callback requested
Reference CR-2026-0148 · Show contact
I'll take it

Reach's side of this is three jobs, all on Reach › Hand devices and Reach › Settings:

  • Enrolling handsets — actually done by the responder on their phone; you just make sure they are allowed to.
  • Proving delivery works — the test alert button.
  • Revoking a handset that has been lost or replaced.

Reach itself never raises an alert. Alerts come from other plugins — Trusted, typically — using Reach as the delivery mechanism.

⚠️
The failure mode of the whole delivery chain is silence. A misconfigured push credential doesn't produce an error anybody sees — it produces a handset that simply never rings. That is why Sending a Test Alert is not an optional nicety.

Before You Enrol a Handset

Four things need to be true. Checking them first turns most "it isn't working" conversations into non-events.

  1. The responder's member record has Telephone Responder on and Responder Certification set to Certified (see Making a Telephone Responder).
  2. Their Personal Email on that record is the address they will sign in with.
  3. They can already sign in to the Reach website on that phone — by provider or by password. If that doesn't work, Hand won't either, and the website tells you why in plain English.
  4. Optional but strongly recommended: Firebase push is configured (next section). Without it Hand still works, but a phone with the app closed will not ring.

Setting Up Push Notifications

Reach › Settings › Hand push notifications

Push is optional, and it is worth understanding exactly what it buys you before you decide. Every handset polls as well as listening, and every alert is stored before any push is attempted — so alerts are never lost either way. What push adds is immediacy, and the ability to ring a phone whose app is closed.

Without FirebaseWith Firebase
App openArrives at the next poll.Arrives at once.
App closed (phone)Nothing until they open it.Rings.
Alert lost?No — it is stored and collected later.No.

Adding the Firebase service account

  1. In the Firebase console, open the project the Hand app was built against and go to Project settings › Service accounts.
  2. Click Generate new private key. A JSON file downloads — this is a credential, so treat it like a password.
  3. In WordPress, go to Reach › Settings and find Hand push notifications.
  4. Paste the entire JSON file into Firebase service account and click Save Changes.
  5. The Status line underneath should now name your Firebase project. Delete the downloaded file from your computer.
  6. Go to Hand devices and send a test alert.
💡
The key is encrypted at rest and never shown again. Leaving the box blank when you save leaves the stored key alone — so you can change other settings freely. To remove one entirely, tick Remove the stored service account.

What each kind of handset can do

HandsetAlert while the app is closed
AndroidYes. Hand raises a full-screen, alarm-category notification, so the phone rings like a call.
iOSYes, but the sound is capped at about 30 seconds — a closed app runs no code of its own, so the system plays it.
Windows / macOSNo push. Hand runs as a tray app that starts with the machine and polls, so "closed" means not on screen rather than not running.

iOS critical alerts

The second setting in this section makes urgent alerts sound through the ringer switch and Do Not Disturb — exactly what a duty handset wants.

🚫
Do not switch this on speculatively. Apple grants critical alerts only on application, and without that entitlement in the Hand app's provisioning profile Apple rejects the notification rather than quietly downgrading it — silencing the very alerts it was meant to make louder. Left off, urgent alerts still break through a Focus mode.

Enrolling a Handset

The responder does this, on their own phone. There is no "add device" button in the admin — nothing you could press would prove the phone in question belongs to them. Your part is the checklist in Before You Enrol; theirs is below, and it takes about a minute.

The usual way — sign in with a provider

  1. Install Hand and open it.
  2. Tap Sign in. The phone's own browser opens on the familiar Reach sign-in page.
  3. Sign in with Google, Microsoft, Apple or Facebook as usual.
  4. The browser hands them back to Hand, which is now enrolled. It appears on Reach › Hand devices within seconds.

The other way — email and password

Hand can also take the email and password directly, with no browser involved. This is what the Windows app uses, and it is the fallback anywhere the browser round-trip fails. The password is the one they set through /reach/reset — see How a Responder Signs In.

💡
Both routes end in the same place: a long-lived token stored on that handset, which Reach keeps only as a hash. Signing in on a new phone does not disturb the old one — enrol as many times as needed.

How many handsets one responder may have

Five. A phone, a tablet and a desktop is a perfectly normal set, and the cap only exists so a stack of forgotten enrolments doesn't quietly become a stack of places alerts are still going.

Enrolling a sixth does not fail: the least recently seen handset is revoked to make room. Somebody standing there with a brand-new phone should never be locked out by an old one they no longer own.

Sending a Test Alert

The Send test alert button at the top of Hand devices rings every live handset, right now, through the real delivery path. Nothing about it is special-cased — that is the point. If the test arrives, the chain works.

Send one:

  • after adding or changing the Firebase service account;
  • after a responder enrols a new handset;
  • before the rota starts relying on alerts — a quiet night is not evidence that alerts work.
💡
The test alert carries no personal data, says who sent it, and expires after five minutes — a test still ringing handsets ten minutes later is just a nuisance. Warn the duty responders first if you're testing at an unsociable hour.

Afterwards, look at Recent alerts further down the same page: the test appears there with Acknowledged by filling in as handsets alarm. That is your proof of delivery, not the "Test alert sent" notice, which only means Reach accepted it.

That table shows the ten most recent alerts and its headings sort too — including Acknowledged by, which is the quick way to pull the unanswered ones together after ringing a selection of handsets. It sorts the ten on the screen, not the whole alert history, which is short-lived in any case: alerts are purged an hour after they expire. The responders named there link to their member records, the same way the Responder column does, and one person answering from two handsets is named once.

⚠️
If the notice says the test could not be sent, the alert never left Reach — check the Firebase service account first. If it says it was sent but no handset rings, the break is further down the chain; Troubleshooting walks through it.

Revoking a Handset

Revoking cuts one handset's access dead, immediately. Use it when a phone is lost, stolen, replaced, or sold — cases where the person is fine but that particular device must stop receiving alerts.

  1. Go to Reach › Hand devices.
  2. Find the row — check the Responder, Device and Last seen columns together, since one person may have several.
  3. Click Revoke and confirm.

The handset stops receiving alerts at once and the responder has to sign in again to use Hand on that device. Their website access, their password and their other handsets are untouched.

💡
This is a belt-and-braces control, not the main one. Eligibility is re-checked against the member record on every request, so somebody who stops being certified stops receiving alerts whether or not anyone remembers to come here. Revoking answers the one question that automatic check can't: "that handset, now."

Alerts & Acknowledgements

The bottom of Hand devices lists the ten most recent alerts: when, what kind, which plugin raised it, the title, whether it was urgent, and — the column that matters — Acknowledged by.

Acknowledged byMeans
One or more namesThose responders' handsets alarmed and they confirmed it. The alert reached somebody.
Nobody yetNo handset has confirmed. Either it has only just gone out, or nothing is arriving — worth a test alert if the pattern repeats.

Alerts go to the whole rota by default, so the first responder free picks it up; an alert may also be aimed at one responder. Either way it is stored before any push is attempted, and what a handset has dealt with is recorded here rather than on the phone — so a responder who reinstalls the app or restores from a backup neither re-alarms for everything nor silently misses anything live.

Where alerts come from

Reach raises none of its own. Other plugins send them — Trusted raising an uncovered shift is the usual case — through a small function that Reach provides. That is a developer-facing detail; the part that concerns everyone is the rule below.

🚫
Never put personal data in an alert's subject or message. That text travels through Google's push infrastructure and lands on a lock screen anyone nearby can read. A caller's name and number belong in the alert's separate contact field, which is encrypted, never pushed, never shown on a lock screen, and audited every time a responder taps Show contact.

That is why the phone mock-up earlier shows a callback alert as a reference number with a Show contact link rather than the caller's details: the responder fetches them deliberately, over an encrypted connection, and the fetch is recorded.

The Other Reach Screens

Not part of responder or device set-up, but the same menu — briefly, so nothing is a mystery.

Call attempts

Every "Spoke", "No answer" or "Wrong / bad number" a Reach user recorded after being shown a member, with filters by member, viewer, outcome and date. It is deliberately read-only: the audit log already records these as having happened, and an edit button would be a quiet way to tidy away an uncomfortable pattern. A correction is made by logging a fresh attempt, not by rewriting an old one.

Call requests

Out of hours, the finder offers Request a callback instead of ringing a 12th stepper directly. Each request is emailed to the address in Settings, and listed here so it can be marked completed once actioned. Caller details are sensitive — deal with them and mark them done rather than letting them sit.

Find a 12th Stepper

The finder itself, inside the admin: enter a postcode or area and see the nearest available members, exactly as a signed-in visitor would. Useful for answering "why can't anyone find me?" without needing a Reach sign-in of your own.

Personal Data & Auditing

Reach hands out members' mobile numbers and email addresses to people it has only verified by email address. That is the whole point of it, and it is also why so much of it is logged.

  • Every member shown by the finder produces an audit entry naming the signed-in visitor who saw them, viewable in Scrutiny's audit log. So "who saw this member's mobile, and when" is always answerable.
  • Recorded call attempts are audited the same way, with the outcome. The caller's free-text note is never written to the audit trail — it appears only on the call-attempt detail screen.
  • Reading a caller's contact details from an alert in Hand is audited, every time.
  • Callback requests are emailed rather than kept, so caller details live in that inbox rather than in the database, and requests are removed after a retention period.
  • Reach creates no WordPress accounts. A signed-in visitor is a signed cookie that expires after 12 hours, holding their verified email and nothing else.
⚠️
Worth telling responders about: the finder shows a coarse badge — Reached recently, No recent reply, Number may be out of date — beside each member, based on what other callers have reported over the last fortnight. No counts, no dates and no caller identities are shown, but members should hear about it from you rather than discover it.

Troubleshooting

A responder can't sign in to Hand, but the website works

That is Hand's stricter gate doing its job. Open their member record and check Responder Certification is Certified — Applied, In Training and Pending are all refused — and that Telephone Responder is on.

A 12th stepper who is not a responder will never get into Hand. That is the intended difference, not a fault.

A responder can't sign in anywhere

Check the Personal Email on their member record matches the address they are actually signing in with, character for character. A different work address, or a Facebook sign-in where they declined to share their email, both look like a stranger to Reach.

A handset isn't ringing

Work down the chain in this order:

  1. Is the responder still certified? Nothing is delivered to a handset whose owner no longer passes the gate — and the row still says Live.
  2. Does the row say Revoked? They sign in again on the handset.
  3. Is Last seen recent? If it is days or weeks old the app is not running at all — reinstalled, phone replaced, or notifications disabled at the OS.
  4. Does Delivery say Poll on a phone? See the next card.
  5. Send a test alert and watch Acknowledged by.

A phone shows "Poll" instead of "Push"

Poll is correct and expected on Windows and macOS. On a phone it means Reach has no working push registration for that handset — usually because Firebase isn't configured at all (check Settings), or because the responder denied the notification permission when the app asked.

Have them open Hand and check its notification permission in the phone's settings; the app re-registers on its own once it is granted, and the column flips to Push.

The test alert says it couldn't be sent

The alert never left Reach. The usual cause is a Firebase service account that is missing, malformed, or belongs to a different project than the app — re-paste the whole JSON key file and check the Status line names the expected project. The Reach log holds the specific reason.

Everyone's handsets stopped working at once

If nobody's handset works and nothing on the member records changed, the site's authentication salts have almost certainly been rotated — which invalidates every enrolled handset by design. Everyone re-enrols; there is nothing to repair.

A handset is listed against an email instead of a name

Reach shows the email when it can't find a member for it — the record was deleted, or its Personal Email was changed after enrolment. If the member is still active, put the address back (or have them re-enrol with the new one); if they have gone, revoke the handset.

The Reach menu is missing entirely

Either your account lacks the personal-data permission, or the Unity or Scrutiny plugin is inactive — Reach needs both and does nothing without them. Ask your site administrator.

Somebody isn't showing up in the finder

The finder lists 12th steppers, not telephone responders. Check Is Twelfth Stepper? is on in the Home group, and that their Areas and Accepts are filled in — a member with no area can't be placed on the map. The Find a 12th Stepper admin screen reproduces exactly what a visitor would see.
